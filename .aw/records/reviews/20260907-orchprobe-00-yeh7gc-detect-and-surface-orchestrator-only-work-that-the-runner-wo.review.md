rejected-linter-shape record is accurate: `ipd_lint.py` contains zero occurrences of "orchestrator" and
`TheRejectedShapeWasNotTaken` pins it. The insistence that the probe be semantic rather than syntactic,
and that a false clear is worse than no probe, are both correct and are the plan's best judgements.

BLOCKER ONE: TWO REQUIREMENTS OF THIS SET COULD NOT BOTH HOLD, and the contradiction was invisible
because each lived in a different plan. Completion criterion 3 requires the refusal be readable in `aw
runs`, which reads durable run state. Child 03's E-04 requires the gate be sited so "a refusal creates no
run directory". Measured in `oc_runipd.initialize_run`: the draft-admission gate raises at `:2791` and the
mixed-type gate at `:2871`, while `run_dir.mkdir` is at `:2880`, and the durable verdict records those
same gates keep are written at `:3054-3075`, i.e. AFTER the directory exists. So a pre-queue refusal is
stdout-only by construction, and `aw runs` would have shown nothing. Worse, the Set's own sequencing
rationale ("shipping the probe before the surfacing would produce a gate whose refusals nobody sees")
silently assumes durability, so under E-04's siting child 01 would have been sequenced first to feed a
surface the refusal never reaches. Resolved as D-1 toward DURABILITY, on the plan's own recorded intent
("not only in stdout at the moment it happened"), and bound on the children by a rewritten criterion 3
plus a new CID-5 that requires `aw runs` output read AFTER the refused run exited.

BLOCKER TWO: THE CROSS-IPD CHECK PINNED THE PROBE TO A FALSE NEGATIVE. CID-2 required that the five live
orchestrators carrying checklists NOT be flagged. But the plan's own F-4 says their items split into
legitimate orchestration and parent-only work, so a probe that clears all five is not a correct probe, it
is a broken one, and CID-2 would have been the artifact certifying it. Classified per item: `5e4sb6` E-01

categories AGENTS.md names as forbidden on a parent (a deliverable, a baseline before any child, a records
reconciliation). Only `cczotj` is orchestration-only, and only because a maintainer ruling on its OQ-04
already moved its verification into child `3v7wo6`, which is the very remedy this Set's refusal must

THE SELF-REFERENTIAL DEFECT, and the one I would most want a reader to notice: this plan violated the
rule it exists to enforce. It carried NO checklist of its children and described that as compliance
("This orchestrator holds ORCHESTRATION ONLY... It declares no `E-*` items"). AGENTS.md says the opposite
in the paragraph this Set graduated from: a parent SHOULD carry a checklist of its children, because most
Sets are executed by a human telling an agent "execute `<setid>`" with no runner, and deleting that
checklist causes the lost work the rule protects against. A blunt no-items linter rule was built and
REVERTED on 2026-09-07 for exactly this reason, and this plan records that revert in its own conventions
while embodying the reverted reading. Fixed by adding E-01 (sequence the three children, stop on the
first that does not reach `executed`) and V-01, with the gate now explaining WHY that retirement stays
honest: the runner performs the ordering itself, so E-01 is genuinely performed-by-the-runner, whereas a
deliverable parked here would not be.

TWO MEASUREMENTS THE PLAN REPORTED AND I FOUND WRONG. The corpus ratio understates the problem by 3x:
46 of the 47 plans carrying `- Kind: orchestrator` carry checklist items (98 percent), not 35 percent. The
130 denominator is the filename-`-00-` population, and 84 of those are pre-`Kind` legacy plans carrying

mutable plan statuses. An executor trusting the old note would have dismissed real failures as known, in
the very module CID-1 reads.
