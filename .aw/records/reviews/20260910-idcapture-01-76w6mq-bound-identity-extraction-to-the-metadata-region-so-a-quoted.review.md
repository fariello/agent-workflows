THE CORE DEFECT IS REAL AND I RE-PROVED EVERY PART OF IT rather than trusting the authored measurements.
`_ID_RE` is still `(?m)^- Id:\s*([0-9a-z]{6})\s*$` applied to a whole body (`selectors.py:111`). On the
`27rjro` research doc, `_read_id` returns `uyeko5`, `_read_status` returns `reviewed` and `_read_setid`
returns `runflags`, all three harvested from a QUOTED IPD metadata block while the doc's own YAML front
matter says `id: 27rjro`. `aw set reviewed uyeko5` fails at exit 2 with "id6 collision matching multiple
files (a data bug to fix, not overridable by `--force`)". The diagnosis, the decision to fix the reader
rather than the document, and the choice to bound all three readers together are all correct.

THE PLAN'S PROBLEM IS ITS EVIDENCE, NOT ITS DESIGN. Three of its own findings (F-4, F-5, F-6) are
falsified, and they are not incidental: they carry the plan's claim that the repository detector had gone
BLIND through retired-record masking, which the plan then uses to argue E-05's rule is "load-bearing rather
than cosmetic" and repeats in its approval gate as the thing "a reviewer should note". Re-measured,
`aw check all` reports exactly ONE `check.id6-collision` and it is ON the `27rjro` doc, naming a SECOND
live research doc (`takpys`) that quotes the same block. Both sides are scannable; nothing is masked;
check and the verbs AGREE. The retired-record mechanism is real (the executed `uyeko5` plan is genuinely
excluded from the 104 plans scanned) but LATENT. I marked those findings SUPERSEDED in place rather than
deleting them, because the plan's prose leans on them and a silent deletion would leave a reader unable to
see that the premise was tested and dropped.

THE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL SINGLE FIX IS A SEVERITY LABEL. E-05 was told to register the new rule at
`warning` severity, with the explicit reasoning that an error would "make the repository fail its own check
for documenting itself". That reasoning is right and the label defeats it: `drift_exit_code` returns 1 for
`error` AND `warning`, and 0 only for `info`. So the instruction as written would have produced exactly the
false-positive gate failure it was written to prevent, on two legitimately-quoting documents, and the
codebase already carries two `info` precedents for precisely this detect-and-nudge purpose.

WHAT REVIEW CHANGED. Eight findings, all FIXED in place, no deferrals and no open questions. Two are HIGH
(the severity label that defeats its own goal; a blast radius of two files where the plan says one, which
would have shipped a half-built rule and a half-built test). Three are MEDIUM (falsified findings the plan
argues from; a 4096-byte header interaction that is the common case rather than an edge case; a wrong suite
baseline whose real failure invites deleting a co-worker's files). Three are LOW (a precedence assertion
that cannot hold, a stale status value, stale counts and line numbers). Both open questions were resolved
from evidence rather than left for the maintainer. E-item and V-item counts are unchanged at seven each.
