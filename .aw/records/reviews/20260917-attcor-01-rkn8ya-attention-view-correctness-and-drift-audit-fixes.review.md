literally would remove a working user-facing flag and break a passing test, i.e. this plan as written
carried a regression instruction. That is PR-014 and it is why the whole finding list needed re-deriving
rather than spot-checking.

### Reversibility note

Every decision above is `Reversible: yes`, and that is a claim worth one sentence rather than silence. Each one changes only PLAN TEXT that no other artifact depends on, so a later maintainer who disagrees edits the plan and re-reviews. D-3 is the closest call: KEEPING a shipped alias is trivially reversible (a future plan may still deprecate `--arcive-state` deliberately, with a deprecation path), whereas the rejected alternative of DELETING it was the irreversible direction. My first pass recorded `no` on that row, having scored the alternative I rejected rather than the choice I made; `check.review-decision-unescalated` flagged it, which is exactly the backstop that rule exists to be. Corrected, and I raised the substance with the maintainer in the final report regardless, since a regression instruction sitting in an approved-track plan is the highest-value thing this review found.
