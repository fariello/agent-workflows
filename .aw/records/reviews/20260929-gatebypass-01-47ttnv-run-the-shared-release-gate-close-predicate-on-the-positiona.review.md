confirming the plan's "fifth caller, never a reimplementation" framing. F-07's docstring text is
verbatim in `runner_shared.close_backlog_item`. F-08's cited test genuinely does not exist. E-01's
stated insertion point is exactly where the plan says (after the `validate_transition_allowed` loop
that prints `Validation error on <name>`, before the `_plan_executed` delegation, before
`is_dry_run`, before `apply_status_change`), and E-02's premise is confirmed: the `decide_gate_default`
and `set_blocks_release_line` writes live INSIDE `apply_status_change`, which runs after that
pre-flight. E-05's managed-block boundary is confirmed by line numbers (`/aw:block` at `AGENTS.md:125`,
the close-legitimacy paragraph at `:225-231`) and the text is absent from `engine.py`, so an in-place
edit is correct. E-06 is confirmed: `--evidence` is declared on `specs set` but not `backlog set`.

THE ONE BLOCKER IS WHAT THE PLAN DID NOT SAY: ITS OWN FIX BREAKS AN EXISTING TEST IN THE DEFAULT BARE
SUITE, so as authored it could not satisfy its own V-07. Worse, a SIBLING PENDING PLAN had already
measured this and taken a dependency that closed a deadlock around it. That is PR-101, and it is the
finding that justified this review.

WHAT REVIEW ADDED BEYOND THE BLOCKER. Two of the plan's own numbers were wrong (a test-count and a
misattributed deliverable), one stated rationale was wrong while its instruction was right (PR-104,
where trusting the reason rather than the instruction would have sent an executor hunting a
nonexistent alias bug), and one open question the plan escalated to the maintainer had ALREADY been
answered by the tree in the four days between authoring and review (PR-106). The bypass-dependency
survey was extended to the whole test corpus and came back clean apart from the one case, which is
what makes E-08 a bounded one-line fix rather than the first of many (PR-102).
