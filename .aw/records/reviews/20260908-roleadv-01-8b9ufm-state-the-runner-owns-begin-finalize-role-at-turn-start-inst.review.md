WHAT THIS ROUND CHANGED ABOUT THE PLAN'S SHAPE. One thing, and everything else follows from it. The
plan treated "who performs the lifecycle transition" as a CONSTANT of the system when it is a RUN
OPTION, and every defect above except the accuracy ones is a consequence of that single mistake: the
statement was unconditional (PR-001), the refusal was drifting toward promising an outcome that
depends on a later gate (PR-002), and the justification for choosing the prompt builders over
`isolation_notice` rested on the unconditionality (fixed in the Under-scope bullet, where the real
reason turns out to be better: `isolation_notice` returns `""` for a non-isolated turn the driver
still finalizes, and the verifier prompt never includes that block at all). The corrected shape is
narrower and more honest: the prompt states OWNERSHIP, derived from the same frozen option the driver
itself reads, and promises no OUTCOME. That is strictly more useful than the original, because an
agent under `--no-self-finalize` now learns the opposite and equally important fact by the statement's
ABSENCE, and the plan's own OQ-01 shrinks from "we cannot know who owns this" to the genuinely
unknowable residue of "we cannot know whether the owner will succeed".
