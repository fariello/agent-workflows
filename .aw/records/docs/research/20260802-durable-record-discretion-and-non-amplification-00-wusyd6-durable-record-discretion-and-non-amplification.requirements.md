# Requirements for Durable-Record Discretion and Non-Amplification in `agent-workflows`

## Purpose

Implement a repository-wide discretion policy that prevents coding agents from
turning private context, personal circumstances, tentative ideas, disagreements,
mistakes, rejected statements, internal rationale, or correction discussions
into durable records.

This requirement applies to every workflow installed or generated by
`agent-workflows`, regardless of which agentic host or model is running it.

The implementation must treat creation of a durable record as consequential
even when the record is not public. A record may be searchable, discoverable,
shared, audited, quoted out of context, retained indefinitely, or made visible
to people for whom the underlying discussion was never intended.

## Problem statement

People are reporting a recurring failure mode in agentic programming:

1. A person shares context so the agent can understand or perform a task. The
   context may concern a private need, personal circumstance, disagreement,
   mistake, hypothetical, tentative plan, sensitive rationale, or concern.
2. The agent mistakes relevance to its reasoning for relevance to the project
   record and writes the context, an inference from it, or an overstated version
   of it into one or more durable artifacts.
3. Someone objects, corrects the agent, or explains why the material should not
   be in the record.
4. The agent then records the correction discussion, repeats the original
   disclosure, attributes it to a person, describes the objection, or implies
   that someone is concealing or retracting something.
5. The attempted correction creates a second-order disclosure that may be more
   intrusive, stigmatizing, accusatory, or damaging than the initial mistake.
6. Additional “fix” commits spread the issue across more history and surfaces
   instead of containing it.

This is not merely a publication, secret-scanning, or PII-pattern problem. Most
affected text is ordinary natural language, and some facts become sensitive
only in context. The failure is semantic and relational:

- treating information disclosed for one purpose as suitable for another;
- failing to exercise discretion about what belongs in a record at all;
- converting tentative language into a factual status claim;
- confusing explanation given to the agent with content intended for users;
- recording why a bad statement was removed instead of producing a clean final
  state;
- creating a record that someone objected, retracted, concealed, disputed, or
  asked to remove sensitive information;
- inferring motives, emotions, diagnoses, protected characteristics, family
  circumstances, conflicts, blame, or reputational meaning;
- using changelogs and decision records as transcripts of the agent's work;
- writing overly candid, judgmental, private, or unnecessary commit messages;
- failing to distinguish current facts from plans, possibilities, examples, and
  rejected claims.

Regex-based secret and PII scanners remain useful, but they cannot detect this
general class of error by themselves.

## Governing principles

> Information provided to help perform a task is not automatically information
> that belongs in the record of the task.

> Correct the artifact, not the history of the conversation. Do not compound an
> inappropriate disclosure by memorializing its correction.

Information may be written to a durable artifact only when all five conditions
are satisfied:

1. **Verified:** It is established as accurate, not inferred, hypothetical,
   tentative, aspirational, or rejected.
2. **Purpose-compatible:** The information was supplied or established for a
   purpose compatible with recording it in this artifact. Mere usefulness to
   the agent's reasoning is insufficient.
3. **Discreet:** Recording it does not expose or invite inference about private
   discussion, personal circumstances, protected characteristics, internal
   deliberation, disagreement, blame, security details, or confidential context.
4. **Necessary:** The target audience needs it for the artifact's legitimate
   purpose.
5. **Appropriate:** It belongs in that specific file or metadata field.

If any condition is uncertain, omit the information or ask before writing it.
Do not ask in a manner that restates sensitive details when a category and
location are sufficient.

Silence is safer than inventing, inferring, or memorializing an unnecessary
claim.

## Contextual integrity and second-order harm

The policy must enforce **contextual integrity**: information may be legitimate
and necessary in the conversation while being inappropriate in source code,
documentation, issue history, a commit message, or any other durable record.

The policy must also recognize **second-order harm**. A later statement can
compound an initial mistake by recording:

- that a person objected to or asked to remove information;
- that a person supposedly wants information hidden;
- that there was a disagreement, correction, accusation, or admission;
- why information is sensitive;
- a more specific inference than the original text supported;
- the agent's judgment about truthfulness, propriety, blame, or motive;
- enough context to reconstruct the original disclosure after its text is
  removed.

The absence of a conventional secret, identifier, or prohibited keyword does
not make such a record safe.

## Threat model and protected surfaces

The policy must cover all durable media, not only public media and not only
source files:

- tracked source, configuration, documentation, and data files;
- `README`, citation, package, manifest, and project metadata;
- `CHANGELOG`, release notes, migration notes, and generated documentation;
- architecture and decision records;
- tracked agent plans, prompts, reports, TODOs, memory files, and logs;
- commit subjects and bodies;
- branch and tag names and annotated-tag messages;
- pull-request titles, bodies, comments, and review replies;
- issue titles, bodies, and comments;
- release titles, descriptions, and attached files;
- generated patches, archives, build artifacts, and published packages;
- content sent to external services, registries, or APIs.

Private repositories and internal systems are fully in scope. Harm may arise
from persistence, discoverability, audience expansion, attribution, or
misinterpretation without any public release.

## Required behavior

### 1. Preserve epistemic status

Agents must preserve the distinction among:

- confirmed current fact;
- completed event;
- approved plan;
- tentative plan or hope;
- possible future option;
- illustrative example;
- hypothesis or speculation;
- rejected or corrected statement;
- private instruction or explanation.

An agent must never promote information from a weaker state to a stronger one.
For example, a possibility must not become a plan, a plan must not become an
event, and an example must not become the selected course of action.

When a statement's status is unclear, the agent must not put it in a durable
artifact without explicit confirmation.

### 2. Do not record conversational provenance

Repository content must describe the project, not the conversation that
produced it.

Do not write:

- what the user privately said, hoped, considered, rejected, or corrected;
- what the agent previously misunderstood;
- why an earlier agent-generated statement was inaccurate, inappropriate,
  deceptive, embarrassing, or unsafe;
- internal debate unless it is an explicitly requested, record-appropriate design
  decision;
- commentary about the user's motives, intentions, emotions, identity, or
  circumstances;
- sensitive reasons a person gave for requesting a feature, accommodation,
  workflow, interface, deadline, exception, or other change;
- the fact that someone objected to, disputed, retracted, or asked to remove
  information;
- an inference that someone wants to hide information or avoid accountability;
- phrases such as “the user clarified,” “we should not claim,” “this was
  misleading,” or “removed because it was not true” unless the user explicitly
  requests that exact statement as part of the legitimate record.

Produce the clean, correct artifact. Do not preserve the correction dialogue.

### 3. Separate functional requirements from sensitive rationale

A private fact may explain why a feature matters without belonging in the
feature specification or history.

Record the minimum functional requirement:

- the behavior the system must support;
- the accessibility or usability property it must provide;
- the constraint the implementation must satisfy;
- the acceptance criterion that can be tested.

Do not record, unless explicitly necessary and authorized:

- who has the underlying need;
- a diagnosis, disability, health condition, family circumstance, protected
  characteristic, personal crisis, workplace conflict, or other private reason;
- speculation about the person's condition or motives;
- that the person asked for the reason to be removed or kept private.

Where a general design rationale is genuinely useful, abstract it to the
least-specific, non-identifying statement that supports the requirement. Prefer
“support keyboard-only operation” over a narrative about why a particular
person needs it.

### 4. Apply strict purpose limits to common files

| Surface | Permitted content | Content that must not be added automatically |
|---|---|---|
| `README` and documentation | Verified facts needed to understand and use the project | Private rationale, tentative plans presented as facts, internal debate, rejected claims |
| Citation and package metadata | Current, verified bibliographic and package facts | Intended venues, speculative status, unconfirmed affiliations, inferred identifiers |
| `CHANGELOG` and release notes | User-visible changes in released or release-ready behavior | Agent activity, correction dialogue, internal cleanup rationale, embarrassing details, ordinary documentation churn |
| Decision records | Deliberate project or architecture decisions that future maintainers need | Conversation transcripts, sensitive personal rationale, disagreements, confidential constraints, rejected falsehoods, routine edits |
| Commit messages | Minimal technical description of the resulting change | Private context, quoted discussion, blame, moral judgment, rejected claims, unnecessary explanation |
| Tracked agent plans/reports | Record-appropriate implementation information intentionally meant to persist | Chain-of-thought-like reasoning, user biography, private strategy, sensitive rationale, speculative claims, hidden instructions |
| PRs, issues, and reviews | Information needed to coordinate the technical change | Sensitive reproduction, private deliberation, unnecessarily specific incident details |

If a file does not need a statement to serve its purpose, the agent must omit
the statement.

### 5. Minimize commit messages

Commit messages are permanent records and may outlive surrounding context. They
must describe the technical delta at the lowest useful level of detail.

Preferred:

```text
Correct project metadata
```

```text
Refine project documentation
```

```text
Adjust feature behavior
```

Prohibited without explicit user authorization:

- repeating the removed or corrected claim;
- describing a private conversation that led to the change;
- explaining that a prior statement was false, misleading, inappropriate, or
  embarrassing;
- identifying who made the error;
- stating that someone objected, asked for removal, or supposedly wants
  information concealed;
- including personal, confidential, security-sensitive, or speculative context;
- using the commit log as an agent diary.

The commit body should be omitted unless it adds necessary record-appropriate technical
information.

### 6. Never create a correction trail for sensitive or unsuitable content

When unsuitable text is found before it has been committed:

1. Remove it from the working tree.
2. Review the full staged diff.
3. Commit only the clean final result.
4. Do not mention the removed text in the commit message.

When unsuitable text is found in an unpushed commit:

1. Stop creating additional commits.
2. Amend, reset, or locally rewrite the affected commit as appropriate.
3. Verify the rewritten history before pushing.
4. Do not create a new explanatory commit that repeats the problem.
5. Do not record that someone requested removal or imply why they did so.

When unsuitable text may already exist in a shared, pushed, released, packaged,
mirrored, audited, or otherwise durable record:

1. Stop committing and pushing.
2. Tell the user which durable surfaces and refs may be affected without
   unnecessarily repeating the content.
3. Ask before rewriting history or changing shared resources.
4. Use an appropriate incident-remediation workflow.
5. Verify all branches, tags, commit messages, historical file versions,
   releases, packages, mirrors, and caches that are in scope.
6. Explain that rewriting Git history cannot erase existing clones, forks,
   mirrors, screenshots, external packages, or downloaded releases.

The remediation summary and commit messages must be content-minimal. Identify
the category, affected locations, and action required without repeating the
private fact, disagreement, allegation, mistake, or correction discussion.

For credentials or other active secrets, revocation or rotation is required;
history rewriting alone is insufficient.

### 7. Require confirmation for record-sensitive content

Before adding or materially changing any of the following, the agent must obtain
explicit user confirmation unless the fact is already present in an authoritative
project source and the task is merely preserving it:

- external submission, review, acceptance, publication, certification, audit,
  approval, partnership, endorsement, sponsorship, or affiliation status;
- unreleased roadmap commitments or target dates;
- legal, licensing, regulatory, compliance, accessibility, security, or privacy
  claims;
- security incidents, vulnerabilities, exploit details, or remediation status;
- personal names, email addresses, phone numbers, locations, account names, or
  other identity information not clearly needed in the record;
- disability, medical, health, accommodation, family, caregiving, financial,
  religious, political, sexual, immigration, employment, disciplinary, or other
  sensitive personal circumstances;
- protected characteristics or facts from which they may reasonably be
  inferred;
- private reasons for a request, feature, exception, preference, constraint, or
  decision when the record needs only the resulting requirement;
- mistakes, disagreements, interpersonal conflict, objections, retractions, or
  requests to remove material;
- confidential organizational, financial, personnel, procurement, customer, or
  contractual information;
- accusations, admissions, blame, deception, misconduct, or reputationally
  sensitive statements;
- inferred intentions, motives, or decisions;
- any statement the user has corrected, withdrawn, disputed, or marked private.

The confirmation question must show, without unnecessarily restating sensitive
details:

- the proposed record or a category-level description when quoting it would
  compound the problem;
- the exact target file or surface;
- whether it will enter Git history, a shared system, or an external service;
- options to omit, revise, or approve it.

Do not scatter multiple confirmation questions when one grouped review can show
all proposed record-sensitive content.

### 8. Treat corrections as a negative instruction and containment signal

If the user says a statement is wrong, private, unsuitable, only an example, or
not for the repository, that instruction applies to:

- the statement;
- paraphrases and stronger or weaker variants;
- explanatory notes about why it was removed;
- commit messages;
- changelogs and decision records;
- generated metadata;
- future edits in the same task.

The agent must search all files changed during the task for related variants
before committing.

The correction also activates a **non-amplification obligation**:

- do not repeat the information while explaining the fix;
- do not describe the objection or removal request;
- do not infer or record why the person wants it removed;
- do not characterize the person's conduct, truthfulness, motives, or emotions;
- do not add a decision record, changelog entry, TODO, issue, or commit message
  about the correction unless a durable incident record is explicitly required;
- if an incident record is required, minimize it to category, scope, impact, and
  remediation without unnecessary personal or conversational detail.

### 9. Apply the clean-counterfactual test

When correcting agent-generated content, ask:

> If the agent had handled the task correctly the first time, would this
> sentence, file, entry, issue, or commit message exist?

If the answer is no, it normally must not be added now.

The desired result is the clean project state that should have existed—not an
accurate chronology of the agent's mistake and correction. This test should
block:

- explanatory commits that exist only because of the agent's earlier error;
- changelog entries describing removal of content that never belonged there;
- decision records memorializing a disagreement that produced no lasting
  project decision;
- statements that someone corrected, objected, retracted, or requested privacy;
- inferences that a person is hiding information;
- new records explaining why an earlier record was unsuitable.

### 10. Apply the person-and-incident red-flag review

Any proposed durable prose about a person, private need, disagreement, mistake,
objection, correction, or incident requires an additional semantic review.
Ask:

1. Can the technical result be recorded without identifying a person?
2. Can the requirement be recorded without the sensitive reason for it?
3. Does the text reveal a fact that was supplied only to help the agent
   understand the task?
4. Does the text create a new fact about the correction itself?
5. Does it imply blame, concealment, diagnosis, motive, emotion, conflict, or
   reputational meaning?
6. Would the statement be surprising, distressing, or misleading if encountered
   later without the original conversation?
7. Does a neutral technical description fully serve the artifact's purpose?

If a neutral technical description is sufficient, use it. If the personal or
incident-specific detail is not indispensable to the record, omit it.

## Mandatory durability gate

Every workflow that writes files, commits, pushes, opens a pull request, creates
an issue, publishes a release, or sends content to an external service must run
the following gate immediately before the durable action.

### Gate A: inspect the complete proposed payload

For a Git commit, inspect at least:

```bash
git status --short
git diff --cached --stat
git diff --cached
```

Also inspect the complete proposed commit subject and body.

If files were generated, inspect their actual generated contents rather than
assuming the source prompt made them safe.

For a PR, issue, release, tag, package, or external API call, inspect the exact
payload that will be sent.

### Gate B: run deterministic scanners

Run configured secret, credential, high-confidence PII, and policy-pattern
scanners against:

- all staged additions and complete new files;
- the proposed commit message;
- other outgoing metadata and text;
- filenames and paths where those can reveal protected information.

This layer should use established scanners where practical. It is a backstop,
not the semantic decision-maker.

### Gate C: perform semantic record-discretion review

Review every newly introduced natural-language claim using this checklist:

1. Is it verified?
2. Is its epistemic status preserved?
3. Was the information supplied for a purpose compatible with recording it
   here, or was it merely context needed to perform the work?
4. Could it reveal or invite inference about a person's disability, health,
   family, identity, protected characteristic, private circumstances, mistake,
   disagreement, objection, or motives?
5. Is it necessary for this artifact's purpose?
6. Can the same functional or technical requirement be recorded without the
   sensitive rationale, person, or incident?
7. Is it in the correct surface?
8. Does it disclose conversation, private rationale, a rejected claim, or a
   correction narrative?
9. Does it record that someone objected, retracted, requested removal, or
   supposedly wants something hidden?
10. Does it infer blame, deception, concealment, diagnosis, emotion, intent, or
    other meaning that is not necessary to the technical record?
11. Does it add record-sensitive content requiring confirmation?
12. Does the commit message reveal information not needed to identify the
   technical change?
13. If this is a correction, would the proposed record exist if the agent had
    handled the task correctly the first time?

The semantic review must examine prose even when deterministic scanners find
nothing.

### Gate D: resolve findings before committing

Classify findings as:

- **BLOCK:** must be removed or corrected before the action;
- **ASK:** requires explicit user confirmation;
- **ALLOW:** verified, purpose-compatible, discreet, necessary, and
  appropriately placed.

The default for uncertainty is **ASK** or omission, never silent recording.

After any fix, rerun the gate on the final staged state and final commit message.

## Required compact always-on instruction

Install a concise managed section in the repository's always-on agent
instructions. It should be equivalent to the following:

```markdown
<!-- aw:durable-record-discretion -->
## Durable-record discretion and non-amplification

Treat repository artifacts, Git messages, tracked plans, issues, reviews, and
other shared outputs as durable records. Information provided to help you
understand or perform work is not automatically information that belongs in the
record of that work.

Record only what is verified, purpose-compatible, discreet, necessary, and
appropriate for the target artifact. Preserve the difference between facts,
events, plans, possibilities, examples, hypotheses, and rejected statements.
Separate a functional requirement from a person's sensitive reason for needing
it. Do not record private circumstances, disagreements, mistakes, objections,
correction dialogue, or inferences about identity, health, disability, family,
motives, blame, concealment, or reputation unless explicitly necessary and
authorized for that record.

Produce the clean final state. If unsuitable content appears, do not compound
it by recording what was removed, who objected, why it was sensitive, that
someone asked for removal, or what someone supposedly wants hidden. Do not
create explanatory commits, changelog entries, decision records, or issues that
memorialize the incident. Stop and contain existing history when necessary.

Before every durable action, inspect the exact content, staged diff, generated
files, message, and outgoing payload. Run configured secret/PII checks and a
semantic discretion review. When uncertain, omit the information or ask using
a category-level description that does not repeat it.
<!-- /aw:durable-record-discretion -->
```

The managed section must be short enough to remain always-on. Detailed
procedures should live in a reusable workflow loaded when the agent is about to
write durable prose, commit, push, or create any shared record.

## Required `agent-workflows` implementation

### 1. Add a reusable workflow

Add a workflow such as:

```text
.agents/workflows/durable-record-review.md
```

It must implement the complete durability gate above and be invoked by every
commit, push, PR, issue, review, release, publishing, durable-document, and
shared-record workflow.

### 2. Route all commit workflows through one safe commit procedure

Create a single canonical commit workflow or command. It must:

1. require a clean understanding of existing user changes;
2. stage only intended files;
3. display and review the staged diff;
4. draft a minimal commit message;
5. scan both the staged content and commit message;
6. perform semantic record-discretion and non-amplification review;
7. request confirmation for **ASK** findings;
8. fix **BLOCK** findings and rerun review;
9. commit only after the gate passes;
10. prohibit `--no-verify` unless the user explicitly authorizes a documented
    emergency exception.

Other workflows must not call `git commit` directly.

### 3. Add repository-local technical backstops

Provide optional, installable hooks using a versioned hooks directory, for
example:

```text
.githooks/pre-commit
.githooks/commit-msg
.githooks/pre-push
```

The installer may configure:

```bash
git config core.hooksPath .githooks
```

The hooks should:

- scan staged additions and commit messages for secrets and configured policy
  patterns;
- reject known private-file paths;
- reject tracked files intended to be local-only;
- warn about unusually long or conversational commit messages;
- block bypass markers or prohibited phrases configured by the user;
- run quickly and explain how to correct a finding.

Hooks cannot perform reliable semantic judgment. They are defense in depth and
must not replace the agent's semantic durability gate.

### 4. Add a pre-push review

Before a push, inspect all commits not present on the upstream ref:

```bash
git log --format=fuller --stat @{upstream}..HEAD
git diff @{upstream}...HEAD
```

Adapt safely when no upstream exists.

The review must examine commit messages as well as file content. A staged-diff
check cannot catch an unsuitable message or file introduced by an earlier local
commit.

### 5. Add CI as a final backstop

Offer a CI workflow that scans:

- the complete PR diff;
- all commit messages in the PR;
- generated documentation and metadata;
- secrets, credentials, high-confidence PII, and configured policy patterns.

CI should block merging or release publication. It must clearly state that CI
is too late to prevent inappropriate recording if a sensitive commit has
already been pushed to any shared remote.

### 6. Keep private scratch material outside tracked paths

Agent reasoning, private notes, correction discussions, and tentative plans
should remain in the conversation or in an explicitly local, ignored location.

If `agent-workflows` offers a local scratch directory:

- create it only with user approval;
- add it to `.git/info/exclude` or an appropriate `.gitignore`;
- verify that it is ignored with `git check-ignore`;
- refuse to use it if Git would track it;
- never assume that a directory beginning with `.` is private.

Do not write private scratch content first and “remember to delete it later.”

### 7. Support repository-specific policy configuration

Allow repositories to extend the policy with:

- forbidden paths;
- local-only paths;
- prohibited literal strings and regular expressions;
- identity and contact-information rules;
- organization-specific confidential terms;
- files requiring explicit approval;
- artifact-specific content rules;
- approved recordable facts or authoritative metadata sources.

The configuration itself must not contain the secrets or private content it is
meant to detect when hashes, identifiers, or safer patterns can be used.

### 8. Make durable actions visible

Immediately before a durable action, the agent should state:

- what surface it will write;
- which files or refs are in scope;
- whether the record will be local, shared, pushed, or externally published;
- whether the durability gate passed;
- any item requiring confirmation.

Do not expose the sensitive text itself in logs or summaries when a category and
location are sufficient.

## Suggested agent interaction for uncertain record content

Use a concise confirmation such as:

```text
Durable-record review

The proposed changes include sensitive personal rationale that is not needed
for the functional requirement. It would enter Git history in two files.

- Record only the requirement (Recommended): Keep the needed behavior and omit
  the personal rationale.
- Revise it: Provide the minimum non-identifying rationale that belongs in the
  record.
- Approve it: Keep the reviewed wording in the listed files.
```

The agent must not quote the sensitive rationale merely to ask the question,
and must not infer approval from the fact that it was disclosed in conversation.

## Test requirements

Add automated workflow or prompt-evaluation cases covering at least the
following:

1. A tentative future possibility is not converted into a current fact.
2. An illustrative example is not treated as the selected plan.
3. A rejected statement is removed without being repeated in a commit message,
   changelog, or decision record.
4. A user's private explanation changes the implementation but is absent from
   repository artifacts.
5. A feature motivated by a person's sensitive circumstance records only the
   functional or accessibility requirement, not the person or circumstance.
6. After sensitive rationale is accidentally recorded, remediation does not
   state or imply that the affected person wants it hidden.
7. A correction made before commit results in one clean commit, not a sequence
   of explanatory commits.
8. An unsuitable statement in an unpushed commit causes the agent to stop and
   propose amendment or rewriting rather than adding another commit.
9. A statement about a mistake or disagreement is not added to a commit message,
   decision record, or changelog merely because it explains the requested fix.
10. An objection or removal request activates containment and does not itself
    become a matter of record.
11. A record-sensitive status claim triggers explicit confirmation.
12. Personal or confidential information is blocked even when it is ordinary
   prose and not a credential.
13. Sensitive context is blocked even when names and direct identifiers are
    absent but identity or protected characteristics can be inferred.
14. A legitimate, verified, purpose-compatible, necessary fact is allowed.
15. A concise technical commit message passes while a conversational message
    with private rationale is blocked.
16. `CHANGELOG` receives user-visible changes but rejects agent process notes.
17. A decision record receives an approved architecture decision but
    rejects conversational and confidential rationale.
18. Pre-push review detects an unsuitable message in an earlier local commit.
19. Generated metadata is reviewed rather than trusted automatically.
20. The workflow does not quote protected content when reporting a finding
    unless the user asks to inspect it.

Tests must include several models and agent hosts where practical. Passing a
single prompt test with one model is insufficient.

## Acceptance criteria

The implementation is complete only when:

- the compact managed instruction is installed in every supported always-on
  instruction surface;
- every durable-action workflow invokes the shared durability gate;
- all commit paths use the safe commit procedure;
- staged content and proposed commit messages receive both deterministic and
  semantic review;
- record-sensitive content requires explicit confirmation;
- changelog, decision-record, metadata, and commit-message purpose restrictions
  are enforced;
- pre-push review covers earlier local commits and their messages;
- correction handling stops the creation of additional contaminating commits;
- optional hooks and CI backstops are available and documented;
- local scratch paths are verifiably ignored;
- repository-specific extensions are supported;
- regression tests cover contextual-integrity failures, sensitive rationale,
  correction narratives, concealment inferences, and second-order harm;
- documentation explains that no control is foolproof and that users must still
  review changes before creating or sharing durable records.

## Priority

Treat this as a high-priority discretion, safety, and repository-integrity
feature. A bad durable statement can harm a person or working relationship even
if it is never public. It can also require history rewriting, release
replacement, package remediation, coordination with collaborators, and
acceptance that existing clones or forks cannot be recalled.

The correct default is conservative:

> Record the minimum verified, purpose-compatible fact needed by the artifact.
> Keep private context and the story of its correction out of the record.
