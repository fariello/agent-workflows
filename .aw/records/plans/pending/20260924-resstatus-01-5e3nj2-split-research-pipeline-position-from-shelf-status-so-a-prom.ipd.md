## Goal

Give research two axes instead of one conflated field: a derived set-level PIPELINE POSITION that answers "was this prompt run, and how far did the fan-out get", and the existing per-document SHELF STATUS that answers "has this answer been ingested, and is it cold". A prompt stops carrying a hot status it cannot substantiate, and every consumer reads the axis that fits it.

TWO AXES MEANS TWO AXES, AND THE PLAN'S FIRST DRAFT COLLAPSED THEM AGAIN AT THE BOARD (F-9, the review's dominant finding). A prompt that is `archive` or `reference` has been DELIBERATELY SHELVED by a human, and that is a SHELF fact; its set's pipeline position is a PIPELINE fact. Mapping position straight onto the attention class makes the pipeline axis OVERRIDE the shelf axis, and measured over the live corpus that resurrects SIX cold-shelved prompts (`uke9sw`, `p8h6ab`, `zsbirf` at `archive` in `occomms`; `75iqeg`, `tsjhfq` at `archive` in `planrev`; `yq6aub` at `reference` in `awoptimize`) from `parked`/`done` onto the live board as ACTIVE, because their sets are `partial`. So the SHELF STATUS WINS WHENEVER IT IS PRESENT: a cold status classifies by the existing `_RESEARCH_MAP` and the pipeline position is never consulted. Position classifies only a prompt with NO shelf status, which after E-11 is exactly the set of prompts that have one.
